Your route to issuing
EICs and EICRs

A practical qualification pathway for an electrician who has passed the 18th Edition and has on-site experience.

!
18th Edition alone is not enough. It demonstrates knowledge of BS 7671, but not full installation, design or inspection-and-testing competence.
Main route

EIC

For a new installation, new circuit, consumer-unit replacement or substantial alteration.

Extra requirements

EICR

Periodic inspection and condition reporting on an existing installation. Follow the EIC competence route, then complete Step 5.

What this teaches: electrical theory, health and safety, installation, termination, design principles, fault diagnosis, inspection, testing and commissioning. The AM2 family is the final independent practical assessment.

2

Keep BS 7671 knowledge current

Your 18th Edition certificate must be checked against the version of BS 7671 you use.

  • BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 is now published.
  • The previous Brown Book route remains valid until 15 October 2026.
  • From 15 October 2026, Amendment 4 must be used.
  • The latest City & Guilds award is 2382-26.

What this teaches: how to locate and apply wiring requirements covering shock and fire protection, circuit arrangements, special installations and verification. It does not teach the complete practical trade.

3

Prove initial-verification competence

This is the inspection and testing needed to complete an EIC.

Included in your core qualification?

Your scheme/provider must confirm it meets the current EAS initial-verification requirement. No duplicate award is normally needed.

OR
Not included or uncertain?

Complete C&G 2391-50 (initial verification) or 2391-52 (combined initial and periodic), or an accepted equivalent.

What this teaches: safe instrument use, continuity, insulation resistance, polarity, earth-fault-loop and RCD testing, interpreting results, inspection schedules and correct EIC completion.

4

Set up the correct certification route

An EIC and Building Regulations compliance certificate are different documents.

EIC signatures

Sign only your competence

You may sign design, construction and inspection/testing only where you are competent for each responsibility.

Domestic England

Join a Competent Person Scheme

NICEIC, NAPIT or another authorised scheme assesses qualifications, work, equipment, procedures and insurance.

Alternative

Building control route

If you are not scheme-registered, arrange building control before notifiable work, or use an authorised third-party route.

Notifiable domestic work: new circuits, consumer-unit replacement and additions or alterations in defined special locations. There is no standalone “Part P qualification”. Commercial and industrial work is outside Part P, but competence, contract, client and insurer requirements still apply.

5

EICR add-on

Complete this step if you also want to inspect existing installations and issue EICRs.

  • C&G 2391-51 periodic inspection, 2391-52 combined, or accepted equivalent.
  • At least two years’ documented periodic inspection and testing experience.
  • Evidence of ongoing continuing professional development.
  • Periodic-inspection registration category when working through a certification scheme.

What this teaches: assessing existing installations, limitations, safe periodic tests, deterioration and defects, C1/C2/C3/FI classifications, overall outcomes and accurate EICR completion. Updated EAS requirements apply to certified businesses from 1 October 2026.

Ready to work within your assessed scope

Before signing independently, have your exact certificates and experience checked by your chosen EWA provider or Competent Person Scheme. Older qualifications can be accepted, but their title, date, content and practical assessment matter.

Guidance summary for England only. It is not a substitute for BS 7671, the EAS Qualifications Guide, scheme rules or site-specific competence assessment.
